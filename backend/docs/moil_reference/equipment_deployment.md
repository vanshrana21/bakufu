## Equipment types commonly used across MOIL mines (deduplicated vocabulary)

**Underground mines** (Balaghat, Ukwa, Gumgaon, Kandri, Munsar, Beldongri, Chikla):
- SDLs (side dump loaders) — capacity cited as 0.66 cu.m at Balaghat
- Tyre-mounted rocker shovels
- Electro-hydrostatic drill machines (piloted at Chikla and Gumgaon)
- Hydraulic sand stowing systems (replacing manual filling, e.g. at Kandri)
- Rock mechanics monitoring instruments (cited at 7 underground mines company-wide, and specifically at Ukwa)
- Large-diameter high-speed vertical shaft sinking rigs (capex-level projects at Balaghat, Gumgaon, Chikla, Ukwa, Munsar)

**Opencast mines** (Dongri Buzurg, Sitapatore, Tirodi, and Kandri's opencast component):
- Drills — 100 mm dia. cited at Tirodi
- Hydraulic shovels — 0.9–1.7 m³ capacity cited at Tirodi
- Dumpers — 20–25 tonnes capacity cited at Tirodi; 20-tonne dumpers cited at Kandri
- Excavators (general mention at Dongri Buzurg, no capacity given)
- Truck-mounted water tankers with sprinklers (dust suppression, cited at Kandri)

No source found any dumper, shovel or drill in the 100-tonne class, or any LHD (load-haul-dump) or true jumbo drill by that name, at any MOIL mine — MOIL's underground fleet vocabulary in public sources runs SDL / rocker shovel / electro-hydrostatic drill rather than LHD / jumbo drill (which are more typical of metal mines elsewhere). Recommendation cards should draw equipment nouns from this list rather than assume standard large-scale opencast metal-mine terminology, since MOIL's disclosed opencast dumper fleet (20–25 t) is markedly smaller than "100-tonne dumper" scale.

### Source productivity summary

The two most productive sources were the **IBM Indian Minerals Yearbook, Manganese Ore chapter** (2020 and 2022 editions, published by the Indian Bureau of Mines) and **MOIL's own Annual Reports** (FY2022-23 and FY2023-24). The IBM yearbooks were the only source that named specific equipment types and capacities at individual mines (SDLs, rocker shovels, drill/shovel/dumper specs at Tirodi, electro-hydrostatic drills at Chikla/Gumgaon) because IBM compiles mine-level operational descriptions across the industry annually. MOIL's annual reports were strong on capex, shaft-sinking projects, and capacity-expansion targets per mine, but did not publish mine-wise production tonnage tables or a fleet inventory in the sections reviewed. A single environmental-clearance executive summary (Kandri, via the Maharashtra Pollution Control Board site) added one more concrete equipment figure (5–6 dumpers of 20 t capacity). MOIL's own moil.nic.in content pages (the "Mines" and "SDU Units at Mine Level" listing pages, which looked most promising for a mine-by-mine roster) returned HTTP 403 errors on every fetch attempt and could not be read in the time available — a person with direct browser access to moil.nic.in may find more there, including the per-mine SDU/compliance PDFs (e.g. `SDU 2023-24 Balaghat Mine.pdf`, `KD-SDU.pdf` for Kandri) that also 403'd on fetch.

**What was NOT findable in public sources:** individual mine-wise annual production tonnage (MOIL discloses only company-wide totals in the AR text reviewed, plus a few forward-looking capacity targets for Balaghat, Gumgaon and Kandri), and any equipment fleet counts (how many dumpers/shovels/drills are actually deployed) for Ukwa, Sitapatore, Munsar, and Beldongri — these four mines have essentially no public equipment disclosure beyond a mine-type classification and, for some, a shaft-depth figure.
